01 · Executive Summary

A wall of frames is a wall of dead ends. We turn each one into a doorway.

ScanMyFrame is a subscription platform that lets frame vendors, photographers, galleries and event creators attach a living digital layer — photos, videos, stories, vendor cards and analytics — to any physical frame they sell, via a branded QR code generated in under 60 seconds.

What we do, in one paragraph

A vendor uploads an image (or images), writes the story behind the piece — optionally with our built‑in AI assistant — and hits publish. ScanMyFrame returns a unique, branded QR code that the vendor prints onto the back of the physical frame. When the buyer, or anyone they show the frame to, scans the code with a phone camera, they land on a beautifully formatted frame page with the full media, the story, and the vendor's contact card. Every scan is recorded against the vendor's dashboard in real time.

Why now

  • Smartphone QR adoption is universal. Native camera scanning on iOS and Android removed the install friction that killed earlier QR products.
  • African creative commerce is going digital. Frame vendors, wedding photographers and galleries across Lagos, Abuja and Accra need a way to stay attached to their work after sale.
  • SaaS infrastructure is now cheap. Supabase, Paystack and CDN‑edge media let us run gross margins north of 80% on a ₦3,000 monthly entry plan.

Why us

We have shipped the platform end‑to‑end — public site, onboarding, dashboard, frame editor, QR generator, public scan page, AI story assistant, billing, admin console — and validated demand with a 2,000+ vendor waitlist. We are now raising to convert that waitlist, harden the marketplace pre‑launch, and expand into Ghana and Kenya.

02 · The Problem

Frames are sold once. The relationship ends at the door.

A frame is the most personal object a vendor can sell — a wedding portrait, a family memory, a piece of original art. Yet once it leaves the workshop, the vendor disappears. There is no way to be remembered, no way to follow up, no way to know whether the piece was loved.

What frame vendors told us

"I have no idea who owns my work."

Frames are hung in homes, offices and galleries the vendor never sees. Word‑of‑mouth referrals depend on the buyer remembering the vendor's name months later — most don't.

"There's no way to keep telling the story."

Every frame has a story — the photoshoot, the commission, the artist's intent. That context lives in the vendor's head, never in the buyer's hands.

"My marketing is invisible."

Without scan data, vendors cannot tell which pieces resonate, which neighbourhoods drive interest, or which seasons sell. They market in the dark.

"Existing tools don't fit."

Generic QR generators give a raw URL with no analytics, no branding, no card and no story. Custom websites cost millions of naira per vendor and never get built.

The market gap

There is no purpose‑built platform for the African frame economy. Vendors string together free QR generators, Google Drive links, WhatsApp Business and a notebook of phone numbers — a stack that breaks every time a link expires, a folder is renamed, or a phone is replaced.

"I linked QR codes to eight frames I sold last month and two customers came back within a week to order more. Seeing where my frames are being scanned changed how I approach marketing completely."
— Adaeze N., Frame vendor, Lagos (early access)

Why this wins

  1. No app required. Native phone cameras scan our codes — zero buyer friction.

10 · Product Principles

Five principles every screen has to pass.

The investor case stops here. From this page on, the document becomes the UX specification — the rules, structure and screen behaviour that make the product feel inevitable to the vendors using it.

1 · Vendor‑first, never tool‑first

The vendor never sees the word "URL", "CMS", "slug", or "embed". They see frame, story, QR, scan. Language is the first product surface.

2 · One screen, one job

Each dashboard tab does exactly one thing. Create Frame creates frames. Analytics shows scans. Settings configures account. No hybrid views.

3 · The buyer is the second user

Public frame pages are mobile‑first, sub‑1.5s load, and never require sign‑in. The buyer experience earns the next vendor.

4 · Defaults beat options

Pre‑filled story prompts, pre‑sized QR exports, pre‑selected privacy = public. Power features (password‑protect, white‑label QR) live behind disclosure.

5 · Brand presence everywhere

Forest green and gold appear consistently on the public site, the dashboard rail, the QR border, the email templates, and the admin console.

6 · Empty states do real work

Every empty state teaches the next action. "No frames yet" is also the first call to Create your first frame — there is never a dead screen.

15 · Design System · Components

The component contract.

Buttons

VariantBackgroundForegroundUse

Gold#D4AF37#0F4C3AHigh‑emphasis on dark — "Start for free" on green sections.

Danger#C5443E#F7F5EEDestructive — Delete, Cancel subscription. Always behind a confirm dialog.

Chips, pills, badges

  • Eyebrow pill — text-[10/12], tracking‑widest, uppercase, cream/forest border, used above every section title.
  • Status pill — Active / Draft / Error mapping to Success / Warn / Error tokens.
  • Plan badge — outlined in plan colour (sage / gold / forest green); used in dashboard, admin Users tab, billing.
  • Coming‑soon pill — gold border on cream — for marketplace teaser and roadmap items.

Form controls

  • Inputs: 12px y, 16px x padding, radius 12, 1px border (theme‑aware), focus ring = 2px primary at 30% alpha.
  • Selects: native <select> styled to match input. Theme‑aware option list.
  • File input: rendered as a button labelled "Upload" or "Change" with helper text ("Only JPG, max 2MB").
  • Toggles: 36×20 capsule, knob 16, gold when on; used for vendor card, monthly/yearly billing.
  • Slider, password‑protect input, copy‑to‑clipboard chip — defined in components/ subtree.

Cards & KPIs

  • StatCard — 16 radius, 1px border, 18px padding, hero variant has 2px accent border + glow at 13% alpha; numeric uses font-display, label is uppercase 10/12 muted.
  • StackCard — vertically stackable cards used on landing testimonials and feature explainer.
  • Plan card — three variants (Basic, Pro, Business) with the recommended one elevated by 2px gold border and "Recommended" tag.

Public frame page (mobile)

  • Hero gallery: max 560px tall image, swipe handlers, opacity dim during swap so height never collapses.
  • Vendor card: sticky on scroll, three taps — WhatsApp, call, email — primary tap is WhatsApp.
  • "Created with ScanMyFrame" attribution: bottom‑centre, 11px, tracks 0.12em uppercase, opacity 50%, links to home.

Iconography

Lucide‑shaped inline SVGs everywhere; emoji is reserved for the About page mission/vision tiles only. Stroke 1.8, linecap round, linejoin round, default size 20.

16 · Accessibility & Performance

The non‑negotiables.

Accessibility

ContrastBody text ≥ 4.5:1 against background in both themes. Forest green (#0F4C3A) on cream (#F7F5EE) = 9.4:1. White on forest = 9.1:1.
FocusEvery interactive element shows a visible 2px focus ring (gold in dark, primary in light). Tab order matches visual order.
KeyboardHeader, sidebar, FAQ accordion, image gallery, plan cards, Frame Editor — all operable without a mouse.
Screen readersHero illustration alt; logo strip title; QR images alt="QR code for <frame title>"; vendor card buttons aria-label.
MotionAll Framer animations respect prefers-reduced-motion; carousels pause on focus; auto‑rotation pauses on click.
FormsEvery input has a visible label; errors announced via role="alert"; required fields explicit, no asterisk‑only marking.
LanguageHTML lang="en"; copy reading age ≤ Grade 8; no jargon for vendors.

Privacy & security

  • Supabase Row‑Level Security on every table; vendor data is invisible to other vendors at the database layer.
  • Login alert email on new device sign‑in (sendLoginAlertEmail, checkAndUpdateKnownDevices).
  • Account deletion is two‑step with a 30‑day grace window; sendDeletionRequestEmail fires an audit notification.
  • Cookie consent banner on first visit; choice persisted; no third‑party tracking before opt‑in.
  • No personal buyer data collected on the public frame page beyond an opaque visitor UUID.
